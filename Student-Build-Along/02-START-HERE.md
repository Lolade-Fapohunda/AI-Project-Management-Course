# Student Build-Along: START HERE

## Welcome

This optional Build-Along gives you hands-on experience applying Artificial Intelligence (AI) Project Management concepts to an AI product.

You do not need to be an engineer to complete the Build-Along.

The experience is designed to help you understand how Product Management (PM) decisions become real AI product capabilities.

You will work through the lifecycle of an AI product from problem definition through evaluation, governance, testing, release, measurement, and continuous improvement.

---

## Two Ways to Participate

### Option 1: Follow the Reference Project

Follow the instructor's PolicyAssist AI example throughout the Build-Along.

This gives you a complete example of an AI product moving through the project lifecycle.

### Option 2: Apply the Process to Your Own AI Project

You may use your own AI product or business use case instead.

Apply the PM decisions, requirements, evaluation, governance, testing, User Acceptance Testing (UAT), release, Key Performance Indicator (KPI), and monitoring activities to your own project.

You do not need to recreate the instructor's technical application.

## Student Completion Sequence

Complete the Build-Along milestones in order.

Each milestone builds on decisions, evidence, and deliverables from the previous milestone.

### 1. Foundation

**Milestone 1: AI Project Foundation**

Understand the current product, its purpose, users, and limitations.

**Transition:**
You have established the current state of the product. Next, you will define the problem it needs to solve and the people it needs to serve.

---

### 2. Problem Definition

**Milestone 2: Problem, Users & Stakeholders**

Define the business problem and identify the needs of users and stakeholders.

**Transition:**
You now understand the problem, users, stakeholders, and desired outcomes. Next, you will translate those needs into measurable product requirements.

---

### 3. Requirements and Planning

**Milestone 3: Requirements**

Translate stakeholder needs into measurable product requirements.

**Milestone 4: Backlog & Minimum Viable Product (MVP)**

Prioritize capabilities and define the initial Minimum Viable Product.

**Transition:**
You now know what the product needs to accomplish and what should be built first. Next, you will examine how the AI solution can deliver those capabilities.

---

### 4. AI Solution Design

**Milestone 5: AI Architecture**

Understand how the major components of the AI solution work together.

**Milestone 6: Data & Retrieval**

Evaluate the quality, authority, currency, and retrieval of the information used by the AI.

**Transition:**
You now understand how the solution works and whether it can retrieve appropriate evidence. Next, you will determine whether the AI can turn that evidence into reliable responses.

---

### 5. AI Quality and Risk

**Milestone 7: AI Evaluation**

Evaluate AI-generated responses and citations using defined test scenarios, quality targets, and Pass/Fail rules.

**Milestone 8: Security & Governance**

Evaluate security, access, privacy, governance, and responsible-use requirements.

**Transition:**
You now have evidence about AI quality and the controls needed to manage risk. Next, you will validate the product more broadly against its requirements and intended user experience.

---

### 6. Product Validation and Delivery

**Milestone 9: Testing & User Acceptance Testing (UAT)**

Validate whether the product meets requirements and is ready for intended users.

**Milestone 10: Release & Deployment**

Evaluate release readiness and plan for deployment, rollback, and operational support.

**Transition:**
You have validated the product and assessed whether it is ready to move toward release. Next, you will measure whether the product is delivering its intended value and performance.

---

### 7. Measurement and Demonstration

**Milestone 11: KPIs & Dashboard**

Measure project delivery and AI product performance using targets, actuals, trends, thresholds, and actions.

**Milestone 12: Capstone & Portfolio**

Bring your work together into a professional AI project portfolio.

**Milestone 13: Final Assessment**

Demonstrate what you learned and applied throughout the Build-Along.

**Transition:**
You have moved from understanding and defining the product to evaluating, securing, testing, releasing, measuring, and demonstrating it.

---

## The Overall Progression

The Build-Along follows this progression:

**Understand → Define → Design → Evaluate → Secure → Test → Release → Measure → Demonstrate**

Each stage answers a different PM question:

| Stage       | PM Question                                             |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------- |
| Understand  | What exists today?                                      |
| Define      | What problem are we solving, and for whom?              |
| Design      | What does the product need to do, and how will it work? |
| Evaluate    | Does the AI produce acceptable results?                 |
| Secure      | Can it operate safely and responsibly?                  |
| Test        | Does the product meet its requirements and user needs?  |
| Release     | Is it ready to move into operation?                     |
| Measure     | Is it delivering the intended value?                    |
| Demonstrate | Can I show and explain what I accomplished?             |

## How to Move Through Each Milestone

For each milestone:

1. Complete the activities in order.
2. Record your observations and PM decisions.
3. Complete the required deliverable.
4. Save your work.
5. Review the PM Checkpoint.
6. Move to the next milestone.

Later milestones may ask you to use requirements, decisions, risks, evidence, or test results from earlier milestones.

Do not discard your earlier work. It becomes part of your overall AI project story.

---

## Testing vs. PM Assessment

Not every activity in the Build-Along requires you to test a feature in the application.

Some activities involve **hands-on testing** of capabilities available in the student application.

Other activities ask you to **assess or define production requirements** for capabilities that may not yet be implemented.

For example, you may be able to test whether the current application appropriately refuses an unsupported question.

You may not be able to test production role-based access controls if authentication and user roles are not implemented in the student application.

When a capability is not implemented:

* Do not claim that you tested it.
* Identify the requirement or control that would be needed.
* Define how the capability should be tested in a production implementation.
* Record the associated risk or PM recommendation.

This distinction is an important part of managing AI products responsibly.

## Build-Along Success Standard

The goal is not simply to complete every table.

Your objective is to demonstrate that you can:

* Make informed PM decisions
* Connect technical behavior to business outcomes
* Identify risks and trade-offs
* Evaluate evidence
* Challenge AI quality claims
* Define appropriate controls
* Determine readiness
* Recommend corrective action when needed
* Explain why you reached a particular decision

When you identify a problem, do not simply record that it failed.

Ask:

> **Why does this matter, what evidence supports the finding, and what should the PM do next?**

## Your Goal

By the end of the course, you should be able to explain and demonstrate how an AI product moves from:

**Business Problem → Requirements → Planning → Build → Evaluation → Governance → Testing → UAT → Release → Monitoring → Continuous Improvement**

The goal is not to become an AI engineer.

The goal is to become an **AI Project Manager who understands enough about the technology to manage the product effectively, evaluate its quality, govern its risks, and make evidence-based decisions.**
